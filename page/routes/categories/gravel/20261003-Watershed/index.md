The Skyline Watershed Ridge Trail is one of the Bay Area’s newest opportunities to explore the Peninsula by bicycle — and for gravel and mountain bike riders, it is an unusually accessible way to get into a beautiful stretch of protected watershed. <br>

Opened to the public in August 2026, the trail follows roughly six miles of ridgeline through the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission’s Peninsula Watershed, beginning near the intersection of Highway 92 and Skyline Boulevard (Highway 35). It is currently an out-and-back route, making the full ride approximately **12 miles round-trip.**

What makes this ride particularly appealing is the scenery. The route passes through a surprisingly varied landscape of **redwood groves, Douglas fir forest, grasslands, coastal scrub and open ridgeline,** with views across Crystal Springs Reservoir, the Peninsula and, in the right conditions, toward both San Francisco Bay and the Pacific. 

One of my favorite vistas is looking down on the Highway 280 and Highway 92 interchange. Seeing familiar Bay Area infrastructure from high on the ridge gives you a sense of just how close this trail is to civilization—even while the ride itself feels remarkably removed from it.

That accessibility may be one of Skyline Watershed Ridge Trail's biggest strengths. Riders can reach the area relatively easily from San Francisco, the Peninsula and South Bay, while Half Moon Bay and other Coastside communities are just over the ridge. It makes the trail an excellent candidate for a morning gravel ride without committing to an all-day trip deep into the Santa Cruz Mountains.

## A Gravel Bike or Mountain Bike Is the Right Tool

The trail is approximately six feet wide and surfaced primarily with crushed rock. While much of it is relatively straightforward, there are hills, curves and **some genuinely steep sections.** The SFPUC specifically recommends a mountain bike suitable for uneven terrain. Grades can reach more than 20 percent in places, and the north-to-south ride includes roughly **1,500 feet of climbing.**

A mountain bike is an easy choice, but riders comfortable on loose surfaces should also find this an appealing gravel-bike route. I'd recommend reasonably durable tires rather than lightweight road rubber, with tire pressure appropriate for your bike, tire width, rider weight and the crushed-rock terrain.
Bring the usual off-road essentials: **a helmet, gloves, eye protection, water and basic flat-repair equipment.** There is no potable water available anywhere along the trail, so carry enough for the entire out-and-back ride. Weather on the ridge can also change quickly from warm and sunny to cold and windy. 
One important restriction for cyclists: **e-bikes are not permitted.** The SFPUC considers electric bikes motorized vehicles for purposes of this trail. 

## Get Your Free Annual Permit Before Riding

Access is free, but every visit requires a free annual SFPUC trail permit.
Fortunately, the process is simple. The permit is available online, can be used immediately after registering and remains valid for the rest of the calendar year. One permit holder may also bring up to six guests. You do not need to print the permit; the confirmation email or a screenshot can serve as proof if watershed staff ask.   That's a very small barrier to entry for access to an area of the Peninsula Watershed that, until recently, most riders could only look at from Skyline Boulevard.

* Get the free Skyline Watershed Ridge Trail permit and [official trail information](https://www.sfpuc.gov/learning/come-visit/peninsula-trails/skyline-watershed-ridge-trail)

## Arrive Early—Especially on Weekends

One practical issue worth emphasizing: **parking is limited.**. The trailhead has **only 22 standard vehicle spaces,** along with five trailer spaces and two accessible spaces. Parking is first-come, first-served, and the SFPUC already describes weekends as very busy.  Based on that—and my experience there—I'd strongly **recommend arriving before 10 a.m. on weekends.**  Highway 35 and Highway 92 carry fast-moving traffic, and there is no dedicated pedestrian crossing at either highway. If the lot is full, a weekday visit or an earlier start is probably the better choice than parking at Skylawn and running across highway 92. <br>

The trail and parking lot are currently **open daily from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m.**

## The Strange Psychology of an Out-and-Back Ride

And one completely subjective observation: **the ride back felt shorter than the ride out.**
The trail is an out-and-back, so mathematically there's obviously no shortcut waiting at mile six. But exploring the terrain and wondering what might be around the next bend, the return seemed to pass much more quickly.  Whatever the reason, turning around at the southern end didn't feel like the beginning of another six-mile ride. Don't give in before you get to the end!

## Worth Adding to Your Bay Area Ride List

Skyline Watershed Ridge Trail isn't technical singletrack, nor is it a perfectly manicured recreational path. It occupies an appealing middle ground: **a genuine off-pavement ridge ride through one of the Peninsula's most protected landscapes,** close enough to the cities that you can ride it in the morning and still have the rest of your day.  Bring a mountain or gravel bike, helmet and gloves. Carry plenty of water. Get your free annual permit before you leave home.
And on a weekend, set the alarm a little earlier than you think you need to.
The redwoods and  view are worth it.
