Your Fox suspension fork is a masterpiece of engineering, but it lives in a brutal world of dust, mud, and high-velocity friction.  *After a technical descent, feel how hot your suspension gets.*  Simplify maintenance by [using Strava](https://www.strava.com/settings/gear) or [Garmin component logging](https://connect.garmin.com/app/gear) and bring your bike in for its 125 hour recommended service.  Creaking, leaking, binding or mysterious noise from your bike indicate required service. <br>

## Annual Fork Maintenance Benefit

• **Butter-Smooth Small Bump Compliance:** Fresh bath oil and lubricated foam rings drastically lower breakaway friction (stiction). You’ll glide over chatter, roots, and rocks that used to fatigue your hands and arms. <br>
• **Maximized Front-Wheel Traction:** When your fork moves freely, your tire stays glued to the dirt. Sharper cornering, predictable braking, and the confidence to hold your line through chop. <br>
• **Consistent Damper Performance:** Fresh lower leg oil prevents dirt from creeping past the internal seals into your expensive GRIP2 or FIT4 damper, keeping your compression and rebound perfectly dialed. <br>

## Why it matters

• **Kashima coat is pricey:** dirt scratches or wears down the [slippery Kashima](https://blog.fullsendbikes.com/fox-kashima-coat/) anodized coating on your upper tubes. Regular service keeps abrasive grit far away from your stanchions. <br>
• **Extreme ROI:** DIY dust wiper and oil refresh costs around $40 in parts. Worn upper tubes or crown-steerer-unit (CSU) easily surpass $500 to $1,200. <br>
• **Air Spring & Seal Life:** lower chassis service prevent deep-seated debris from accessing air spring seals, preventing mid-ride pressure loss or "stuck down" forks. <br>

## Technical Walkthrough

### Baseline Extraction & Safety
Record all user settings as your structural baseline. Test ride recommended to gather pre-service feel.  Photograph excessive wear, if discovered. Back out all rebound and compression adjusters completely counter-clockwise to prevent internal binding or damage during cycling. Cover the air valve with rag and purge air. Depress the Schrader valve a second time while compressing the fork to ensure no residual air remains trapped in the negative chamber.

### Lower Chassis Decoupling
Loosen the bottom nuts on both the air side and the damper side by exactly 3–4 full threads. Do not back them off completely; leave the nut flush with the top of the shaft threads to protect them. Use a specialized Fox Splash Nut Tool or a deep-well socket to strike the nuts sharply with [a dead-blow mallet.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead-blow_hammer) This unseats the tapered shaft interference fit from the lower leg castings. Remove the nuts and crush washers, then smoothly pull the lowers away from the upper tubes.  Place all items in a magnetic tray to prevent loss.  Invert the fork on a bicycle stand over a oil pan to capture spent oil. 

### Bore Decontamination & Seal Dressing
Lever the old dust wipers out of their seats using the wiper installation tool. Pull the foam rings and look for evidence of oil breakdown or particulate bypass. Clean the internal lower leg bores with 99% Isopropyl Alcohol (IPA) and a lint-free microfiber dowel until the wiping stroke comes out completely clean.  Submerge new foam rings in Fox 20W Gold bath oil until fully saturated. Use the dedicated [Fox Flangeless Seal Driver Tool](https://www.pushindustries.com/products/32mm-fork-seal-driver) to seat the new dust wipers perfectly square with the top of the lower leg bore. Ensure the foam rings sit flat below the wipers without twisting.  Apply wipers to a thoroughly clean surface.  Assure wipers are level.  A distinct tone change occurs when tapping wipers in-place -- *listen for this.*<br>
Replace sag indicator O-ring.

### Micro-Lubrication & Precise Fluid Charging
Lightly coat the inner dual-lip scraper edges of the dust wipers with Slick Honey grease. With the forks perfectly verticle in a bicycle stand, align and slide the lower legs onto the stanchion tubes. Stop sliding immediately after the stanchions pass the dust wipers and foam rings, but before the air/damper shafts penetrate the bottom holes of the casting. Resistence or hesitation when sliding could mean a kinked or torn wiper seal -- *check for this.*<br>
Tilt the fork assembly roughly 45 degrees with the bottom holes facing upward. Using a graduated syringe, charge each leg with its specific volume of oil: <br>
• **Damper Side:** Charge with the chassis-specific fluid (e.g., Fox 5wt Teflon for GRIP/GRIP2 or 20W Gold depending on year/model). <br>
• **Air Side:** Charge with Fox 20W Gold fluid to ensure consistent stanchion lubrication.

### Torque Certification & Validation
Slide the lowers completely down until the threaded shafts extend cleanly through the bottom holes. Wipe away any migrating oil from the external thread faces. Always replace the crush washers with brand new factory units to prevent slow weeping.
Hand-thread the bottom nuts to prevent cross-threading, then torque them using a calibrated digital torque wrench to 5.7 Nm (50 in-lb). Reinstall the rebound adjuster assembly. Pressurize the air system to 50% of the rider's target PSI, cycle the fork 10–15 times through the first 30mm of travel to equalize the positive and negative chambers, then top off to the final recorded PSI. Reset the damper adjusters to their baseline clicks and confirm smooth breakaway performance.

## Service Test

Once assembled, complete static bench tests:<br>
• **Breakaway Stiction:** With the damper open, gently push down on the handlebars.  The fork should smoothly transition into its travel with negligible initial resistance. <br>
• **Indexing & Damper Validation::** Turn the low-speed compression (LSC) and low-speed rebound (LSR) adjusters from fully open to fully closed while cycling the fork.  A noticeable, immediate change in shaft speed and resistance. Rebound should be highly controlled when closed. <br>
• **Knock Test:** Lock front brake, place your hand over the dust wiper/stanchion junction, and rock the bike firmly forward and backward.  Clicking, knocking, or structural play indicates worn lower leg bushings or an unseated damper/air spring shaft. <br>
* **Test Ride:** Bike should feel sturdy, planted, and offer controlled resistence over curbs and jumps.
